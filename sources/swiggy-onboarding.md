## Opening and goals

The piece opens on a warm metaphor — a new user's first experience should
feel like walking into a neighbourhood shop that already seems to know you —
then states three concrete goals driving the redesign: update the visuals to
match Swiggy's 2021 brand refresh, fix existing usability problems in the
signup sequence, and give non-food verticals (Instamart, Genie) more
visibility during onboarding, since the old flow only really sold "food
delivery."

## Research and process

The team benchmarked onboarding flows across many other apps, structured
around explicit questions (how many steps, speed vs. giving context,
which authentication methods), then mocked up and compared multiple
permutations of the sign-up sequence before settling on a direction to build
forward from.

## Design decisions, screen by screen

- **Intro screens:** chose to lead with "hyperlocal convenience" framing
  rather than a generic value-prop or pure brand-recognition pitch, matching
  the broader company push beyond food delivery.
- **Progress indicator:** an earlier, more playful progress visual confused
  users in hallway testing, so the team replaced it with plainer, more
  direct contextual copy about what step they were on.
- **Incentive placement:** a "gift intrigue" screen revealing a WELCOME50
  coupon appears only after phone verification — deliberately timed so that
  returning users who wouldn't actually qualify for the offer never see it
  and get confused or frustrated.
- **Location step:** shifted from asking for exact GPS coordinates to asking
  for locality-level information instead, which better matched what the
  backend actually needed to operate on.

## Rollout and result

The new flow ran as an experiment from July–September 2022, then expanded to
100% of users by October 2022. The team reports real but partially
undisclosed results: forced signup email collection was creating drop-off,
and making the email field optional (since many Indian users don't maintain
one) measurably increased both completed signups and new-user acquisition —
the author states explicitly that exact figures can't be shared publicly.

## Closing

The author frames the central tension they kept returning to as: is the
incentive strong enough to justify the number of steps asked of the user,
and closes with a personal reflection on what the project taught about
balancing stakeholder/business pressure against the user's actual
perspective.
