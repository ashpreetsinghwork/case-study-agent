## Opening

The authors, two design managers responsible for News Feed, frame the project
as an effort begun in late the prior year to make News Feed more readable,
conversational, and easier to navigate for a community of two billion people.
They note the stakes explicitly: because News Feed is so heavily optimized,
even tiny changes (a few pixels of padding, the tint of a button) can have
outsized and unpredictable effects. The trigger for the work was direct user
feedback that News Feed had become cluttered and hard to navigate.

## Section 1 — Improving readability on News Feed

The team says its research and design groups are in constant dialogue with
real users, and that three things consistently come up as what people care
about most in a story: the content itself, who shared it, and how to respond
to it (comment, reaction). From that, they derived three guiding "how might
we" questions — make content easier to read and distinguish, make it more
engaging/immersive, and make giving feedback easier — and ran a cross-team
design sprint (designers, researchers, content strategists) around them.
Concrete directions explored: stronger visual hierarchy and bigger/higher-
contrast type; evolved iconography with larger tap targets; and full-width
content with less surrounding UI clutter. They're explicit that testing
surfaced real failures along the way — e.g., putting text on top of photos,
or dropping explicit text labels, both created new legibility problems that
had to be walked back before arriving at the final layout and typography.

## Section 2 — Making comments more conversational and engaging

Stated briefly: the goal was more meaningful conversation, with conversation
more central to the experience and richer ways to express yourself. They
frame the old comment format as rooted in message-board conventions with
limited room for personal expression, and say that looking at messaging-app
conventions (rather than message-board ones) showed a better path for how
people naturally converse.

## Section 3 — Making navigation between News Feed stories easier

The team observed in lab studies that people would get "stuck" once they
opened a story from the feed, and separately struggled to find a reliable
way back because back-button behavior had been inconsistent across different
immersive views over the years. The fix: consistent back affordances across
every immersive view, removing redundancy between the navigation bar and the
story's own title, animating the transition so content expands in place
(preserving a sense of context), and adding swipe-back gestures.

## Closing

The piece ends on an explicit "nothing is ever done" note — framing the
redesign as a foundation to keep building on rather than a finished state —
followed by an extensive list of team members and leadership (including Mark
Zuckerberg and Chris Cox) thanked by name.
