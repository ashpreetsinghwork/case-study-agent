## Framing

The project is scoped as a 21-day mentored exercise, with the designer
choosing Blinkit's search flow specifically because of industry data: a
cited Adobe study claiming 73% of online grocery shoppers use search as
their primary way of finding products, that search-using customers convert
about 7% better, and that 69% of users would abandon a grocery app entirely
if they couldn't find an item within two minutes. The target users framed
are busy professionals and families who want speed over browsing.

## Problems identified

A heuristic evaluation (Nielsen's framework) surfaced several concrete
issues: the search bar wasn't visually prominent enough, Android voice
search had no way to cancel once started, users often needed multiple
separate searches to complete one purchase, and carousel items had
inconsistent heights that hurt visual coherence.

## Research

Primary research was five interviews with people matching the target
profile, which reinforced that search clarity and navigation speed were the
real friction points. Secondary research pulled in broader industry data and
competitor patterns to corroborate the primary findings rather than drive
the design on its own.

## Solution

Three main additions: a "Recently Bought" section intended to make
reordering faster and lift both average order value and repeat usage; price-
based sorting and filtering to shorten decision time; and a reorganized
layout that puts more useful information above the fold to cut down on
scrolling and cognitive load.

## Validation

The prototype was tested with multiple participants via Maze (a remote
usability-testing tool), which produced task-completion rates, heatmaps, and
qualitative read on how confidently people used the new search results and
filters — described as generally positive, including favorable reaction to
the Recently Bought section. Findings from that round fed a further
iteration before the final prototype.

## Closing note

The writer mentions, as a kind of after-the-fact validation, that Blinkit
itself later shipped features resembling parts of this proposal, and closes
on personal skill-building takeaways (UI craft, communicating design
decisions, and writing clearly about process) rather than quantified
business results, since none were actually measured in production.
